The INA Frameworks™ Suite

Six proprietary methodologies that structure how INA identifies, evaluates, finances and monitors digital infrastructure projects.

The Suite

Six frameworks. One project lifecycle.

Each INA Framework™ works as a standalone methodology, but reaches its full value applied together, across a project's life — from first opportunity screen to post-implementation monitoring. Every framework is designed to evolve into a software module or AI-assisted capability as the INA platform matures.

Flagship Methodology

INA Project Structuring Framework™

The backbone of INA's advisory practice. It transforms a raw digital infrastructure opportunity — a ministry's connectivity ambition, an operator's expansion plan, a fund's mandate — into a structured, bankable, implementable project, following a disciplined stage-gate sequence.

Core Principles

  • Stage-gated, not ad hoc
  • Every project speaks a common structuring language
  • Technical, financial and regulatory feasibility assessed together
  • Designed to be audited by a development bank

Core Dimensions

  1. Opportunity Screening
  2. Technical Feasibility
  3. Regulatory & Institutional Fit
  4. Financial Structuring
  5. Risk & ESG Review
  6. Bankability Sign-off

INA Investment Readiness Index™

A composite index that scores a project from 0–100 across eight weighted dimensions, translating readiness into a single, comparable number that investors and DFIs can act on quickly.

Core Principles

  • Comparable across sectors and geographies
  • Designed to be re-run as a project matures

  3. Financial Model Robustness

  5. Market Demand Evidence

Financing Landscape

Multilateral Finance, in one map

Development finance institutions increasingly prioritize projects that combine public policy objectives with private investment. INA speaks that language on both sides of the table.

  • Multilateral Development BanksLong-tenor, policy-aligned financing for public infrastructure.
  • Development Finance InstitutionsBlended mandates across development and commercial return.
  • Project FinanceNon-recourse structures tied to asset cash flows.
  • Public-Private PartnershipsRisk-shared delivery across public and private balance sheets.
  • Blended FinanceConcessional capital that de-risks commercial participation.
  • GuaranteesCredit enhancement that unlocks otherwise unbankable projects.
  • Export Credit AgenciesFinancing tied to cross-border equipment and services.
  • Commercial & Institutional CapitalBanks and investors once a project clears readiness.
